Every other module in this guide fits somewhere in the model introduced here. Layering is the single idea that turns networking from a pile of unrelated facts into something you can reason about.

## In This Module

- Why networks are built in layers at all
- The TCP/IP model and how the OSI model maps onto it
- Encapsulation, or what actually wraps what as data leaves your machine
- Reading a layer stack from the bottom up during troubleshooting
- Seeing all of it at once in a single packet capture

## Planned Coverage

- Open with a concrete request, such as loading a web page, and follow it down the stack and back up the other side
- Introduce the four TCP/IP layers, then present OSI as the vocabulary people use in conversation and in error messages
- Encapsulation demonstrated visually, frame wrapping packet wrapping segment wrapping data
- Establish the troubleshooting habit early, work bottom up, because a working layer 3 is meaningless if layer 2 is broken
- Close by naming which module covers which layer, so the reader has a map of the guide
